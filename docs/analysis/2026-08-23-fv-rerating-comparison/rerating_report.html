What moved, and why

The discount rate is keyed to market risk (WACC → beta), the MOS to business durability (ROIC−WACC spread). The two levers pull in opposite directions across the risk spectrum.

Method & caveats

Old = static DISCOUNT_RATE = 0.10 / MOS = 0.90 across every leg, plus the pre-Option-A wacc() (no captive-finance floor/cap) for the quality score. New = the live branch: a per-company rate clamp(0.7·0.10 + 0.3·WACC, 0.085, 0.13) with a 9% DDM floor, a durability MOS 0.85 + max(spread/15, (roic5−wacc)/5)·0.10 clamped to [0.85, 0.95], and the Option-A wacc() fix. Both numbers for each ticker come from a single fundamentals fetch, so the delta isolates the model change.

Quality is nearly inert because the Option-A wacc() fix only bites on the captive-finance debt signature — in this database only OKTA and LEU shift (−0.2). Everything else scores identically. FINANCIAL names (JPM, SOFI) keep an identical FV: the rate is invariant for them, and with no ROIC−WACC durability signal the MOS stays neutral.

23 names are unvaluable by both models — pre-profit / early-stage miners, SPACs and foreign tickers (ACHR, OKLO, IONQ, RHM, RR…) where neither the old nor the new engine produces a fair value; their FV cells read N/A. One name (ZETA) hit a quality-metric error and shows no score. SYM's spread is a known ROIC-denominator artifact; its FV delta is still bounded.
